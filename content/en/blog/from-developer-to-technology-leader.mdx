I started as a Software Developer, then became a Tech Lead, and I am now the CTO at TechShield. Each step was not just a new title, but a different way of looking at the work.

## As a Developer

Success was fairly easy to measure: features work correctly, code is clean, bugs are few. Most of my time went into **how** to solve a technical problem.

## As a Tech Lead

The question shifted from "how" to **"what and why"**. A few things I had to relearn:

- **The team's results matter more than mine.** A thorough review or a clear design doc sometimes creates more value than writing an entire feature myself.
- **Technical decisions are trade-offs.** There is no perfect choice — only the one that fits the constraints of time, people and cost.
- **Communication is a technical skill.** Explaining why an architecture was chosen to people who don't write code is part of the job.

## As a CTO

The scope widened again: not just systems, but **product, team and strategy**. I spend more time on questions like:

1. Which product should be built first, and which can wait?
2. Which technical foundation will help the team move fast over the next year or two?
3. How can the team make good decisions without waiting on me?

## What didn't change

Whatever the role, I keep the habit of **touching the code**. Not to do the team's work, but to stay close to reality, keep my technical intuition and make decisions grounded in real understanding.

## Conclusion

The journey is still ongoing. I'll keep sharing the lessons — successes and mistakes alike — on this blog.
